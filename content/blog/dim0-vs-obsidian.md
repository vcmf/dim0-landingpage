## Quick take (read this first)

**The idea in two sentences.** Obsidian keeps your knowledge as plain Markdown
files on your own disk, linked into a personal graph, extended by a huge plugin
ecosystem. Dim0 puts notes, sketches, code, and a board-aware AI agent on a
shared infinite canvas that syncs in real time.

**Where Dim0 wins**
- Real-time multiplayer. Obsidian is fundamentally single-player (Sync is for *your own* devices, not co-editing).
- AI is native, not a plugin: an agent that reads the board, searches, runs code, and writes results back as nodes.
- A spatial canvas with every node type (notes, shapes, code, charts, mini-apps), not just linked documents.
- Opinionated and ready out of the box: the canvas and the AI are built in, versus Obsidian's assemble-it-yourself plugin box (whose visual features are secondary bolt-ons).
- Runs in the browser, nothing to install, and still open-source and self-hostable with Markdown export.

**Where Obsidian wins**
- **Local-first and offline.** Your notes are files on your machine. No account, no cloud dependency, works on a plane.
- A massive plugin ecosystem and deep personal-knowledge-management workflows (backlinks, graph view, dataview, spaced repetition).
- Zero vendor lock-in in the strongest possible sense: it's just a folder of Markdown.
- Free to use, and as of 2026 that includes work/commercial use (the old commercial license is now optional). A fiercely loyal community.

**Pick Dim0 if** you collaborate, you want AI that acts on your work, and you
think spatially.
**Pick Obsidian if** local-first, offline, single-player PKM with total file
ownership and plugins is the priority. That's a real, valid priority, and Dim0
does not try to be that.

## The honest framing

Notion and Excalidraw are close comparisons for Dim0. Obsidian is a more
*interesting* one, because it optimizes for something Dim0 deliberately doesn't:
**your files, on your machine, forever, alone**. So this isn't a "we win" piece.
It's a "here's the fork in the road" piece. If you're an Obsidian purist, read
the "where Obsidian wins" section and take it seriously.

## What Obsidian is (fairly)

Obsidian is a **local-first Markdown knowledge base**. Notes are `.md` files in a
folder you control. You link them with `[[wikilinks]]`, visualize the result as a
graph, and extend the app with a plugin ecosystem that is genuinely enormous
(dataview, templater, spaced repetition, and thousands more). There's a Canvas
feature for spatial arrangement, and paid add-ons for multi-device sync (Sync,
around $4/user/month) and web publishing (Publish, around $8/site/month). The app
itself is free, and as of 2026 that includes commercial use. It is, for many
people, the most trustworthy place to keep a lifetime of notes precisely because
there's no cloud in the loop.

What it is *not* built for: **real-time collaboration** (Sync moves files between
your own devices, it isn't co-editing) and **native AI** (that comes via plugins,
bolted on).

## What Dim0 is

Dim0 is a **real-time collaborative thinking canvas** with AI built in. An
infinite board holds Notion-grade notes, freehand shapes, code, charts, docs,
nested boards, and generated mini-apps, and a **board-aware agent** reads that
board before it acts. It's web-based, runs 8 AI models, and is open-source (MIT)
and self-hostable, with notes stored as plain Markdown so you can still walk away
with your content.

The philosophies rhyme on **openness and Markdown**, and diverge hard on
**local-first-single-player (Obsidian) vs cloud-collaborative-AI-native (Dim0)**.

## Opinionated canvas vs open box

Obsidian's superpower is that it's an **open box**: unopinionated, endlessly
extensible, yours to shape. The catch is that the powerful Obsidian everyone
raves about (the dataview dashboards, the templater automations, the elaborate
"second brain") is a **system you assemble yourself** from plugins and config.
Writing a note takes five minutes; building the workflow is a project. That's the
real learning curve. It isn't the editor, it's the paradox of choice. Dim0 takes
the opposite bet: **opinionated**. The canvas is the interface, the agent is built
in, and every node type works out of the box, so you get value without assembling
a toolchain.

And on visual thinking specifically, be precise: Obsidian isn't blind to it. It
ships a **Graph view** and a **Canvas**. But both are secondary, single-player,
AI-less bolt-ons. The Graph view visualizes your links and tends to become a
pretty hairball at scale rather than a genuine thinking aid; the Canvas lets you
arrange note cards with basic connectors, but with no real-time collaboration and
no agent acting on it. Obsidian's native mode is **text and links**. Dim0's native
mode is the **spatial canvas, with AI on it**. If your thinking is visual, that
difference is the whole ballgame.

## Where Obsidian is genuinely better (take this seriously)

- **Local-first ownership.** Files on disk, offline, no account. Nothing beats it
  for "I want my notes to outlive any company."
- **PKM depth.** Backlinks, graph, dataview queries, years of workflows the
  community has built. Dim0 is not a personal-knowledge-management tool.
- **Plugins.** If your workflow depends on a specific plugin, Dim0 won't have it.
- **Single-player focus.** No collaboration overhead, no cloud, just you and your
  vault.

If those are your top priorities, use Obsidian. Genuinely.

## Where Dim0 pulls ahead

The moment your work becomes **shared, visual, or AI-assisted**, Obsidian's
local-first single-player model becomes the constraint:

1. **Real multiplayer.** Bring a team onto the same board with live cursors and
   conflict-free edits. Obsidian can't co-edit.
2. **AI that acts, natively.** Not a plugin summarizing a note, but an agent that
   reads the whole board, runs code, and builds on it.
3. **A true canvas, not just arranged notes.** Shapes, code, charts, and mini-apps
   as first-class nodes.

And you still keep the parts Obsidian users care about: Markdown notes, open
source, self-hosting.

## So which should you use?

- **A private, offline, lifelong second brain that's just your files:** Obsidian.
- **A collaborative, AI-native canvas where a team (or you) thinks in space:** Dim0.
- They can even coexist: think and collaborate in Dim0, export the Markdown into

Different tools for different jobs. If your notes are a solo, offline vault,
Obsidian is home. If your thinking is shared, spatial, and AI-assisted, that's
Dim0.
